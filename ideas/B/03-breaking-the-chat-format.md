# B3. Breaking the chat format

Session B, task 5. No code, no paid calls. Web pages read on 2026-10-19. Labels: **[User]**, **[Added]**, **[Found]** (with link).

## 1. Seed

[User] "Not 'world's turn, player's turn'. Interruption by button, live and retroactive (already decided); text paced like speech; NPCs keep unsaid remainders as intents. What other interactive possibilities exist beyond these: reacting mid-scene, gestures, quick actions, ambient events."

## 2. Completed idea

### 2.1 One mechanism under everything: the playback clock [Added]
Interruption, speech-paced text, voice (B4) and flowing time all depend on one thing the decisions imply but do not name: a **playback clock** that says what the player has *received so far*. "Said" must mean one thing. Candidates: generated by the model; delivered to the client; displayed on screen; heard in audio. The decided rule "everything after the cut point was never said" only works if *said = displayed/heard at the moment of the cut*. So:
- Generation streams fast; the client holds a **queue** per speaker and releases words at speech pace (text) or at audio position (voice).
- The cut point is a **word index into the delivered stream**, stamped by the client with a timestamp, and sent to the server. Server rolls back extraction past that index (decided) and records "player interrupted X at word Y".
- With voice, word index comes from alignment (TTS returns word timings, or the line is synthesised sentence by sentence and each sentence has a known start time).
- Everything not yet delivered when the cut happens is discarded, including *the generation still in flight* (cancel the call; do not pay for it).

The retroactive window (decided: last NPC utterance only) is the same structure with the cut point chosen from stored text after the fact.

### 2.2 Interactions beyond "type text" [Added]
Grouped by what they cost the engine. Each is a **typed event** with a time stamp and an optional reference to the NPC utterance word index it happened during.

**Free of the generator (cheap, instant on screen)**
- **Reaction taps** during an NPC's line: a small set (nod, shake head, frown, smile, look away, hand to weapon, stay silent). Recorded as "player reacted R at word W". The NPC's *next* call sees it if it was perceptible. These are the gesture channel the seed asks about, with no text writing. They also serve as the non-textual answer to a speaking NPC; a lot of play is "listen and show a face".
- **Hotspots in narration**: long-press a name or object in the story text to *look at / ask about / take*. Only entities the PC has perceived can be hotspots, so the feature cannot become a leak channel (same filter as B2).
- **Quick actions from the panel** (B1): draw/sheathe weapon, drink an elixir, change hands, sit, hide. Typed, no prose needed; the narrator describes the effect.
- **Pause / wait / skip** controls, including "wait until..." a stated condition (flowing mode, see 2.4).

**Need generation but not on the critical path**
- **Ambient events**: a knock, a bell, a drunk shouting outside, rain starting, a rider passing. In flowing time these are what makes silence feel inhabited. They come from the world level (cheap judgments about what is plausible here and now, plus anything the background simulation scheduled), not from the narrator inventing noise.
- **NPC-initiated beats**: an NPC interrupts the player, repeats a question, or leaves. In turn-based mode only at turn boundaries; in flowing mode on a timer.
- **Unsaid remainder surfacing**: an NPC who was interrupted may return to the thought later (decided as a private intent). The player should see only *behaviour*: he opens his mouth, closes it, mutters. The intent itself is not shown (that would expose private NPC state).

**Meta**
- **Rewind / branch** ("undo this exchange", "what if I had said X"): the append-only log makes this nearly free for *state*; the cost is the user's patience. [Found] AI Dungeon's retry stack is the baseline ([help](https://help.aidungeon.io/faq/how-to-play)).
- **Safety verbs** (pause, fade to black, skip, rewind): see B5. [Found] Script Change's vocabulary (pause, fast-forward, rewind, frame-by-frame) maps cleanly onto these controls ([overview](https://crowscorner.bearblog.dev/playing-with-care-a-guide-to-rpg-safety-tools/)).
- **Journal / "what do I know"** view: PC-knowledge log only. Lets the player resume after days away without opening the debug view.

### 2.3 Text paced like speech: details the idea needs [Added]
- **Pace per speaker**, not one rate: a slow, drawling NPC and a quick one differ; pace is a field on the character record plus emotion (anger: faster). Pauses are punctuation-driven, so ellipses and dashes mean something.
- **Skippable and adjustable** (B5): a user who wants to read fast sets the pace high; interruption points still exist.
- **Narration vs speech**: narration might not need speech pace (it is not spoken). Pacing narration is a choice; it keeps the rhythm but can feel slow on a phone.
- **Latency hiding**: the NPC call takes seconds. During that time show an in-world pause beat ("he considers it") generated cheaply or chosen from a small fixed set. Faking never claims a state change; it is presentation.

### 2.4 Turn-based vs flowing: what the interface owes each [Added]
- Turn-based: the world waits; ambient events are only narrated at turn boundaries; interruption exists but nothing "happens" in silence.
- Flowing: silence counts. A **silence timer** (setting) advances time; NPCs act or prompt ("Well?"). Ambient events matter most here. Hard problems:
  - **Phone backgrounding.** If the tab or app is hidden, time must pause (or the world runs on without a player: a bad surprise). This is a rule, not a setting.
  - **LLM latency** vs "world does not wait": the world can only act as fast as calls return; the decision "quality beats cost and latency" means flowing mode will feel slower than a real-time game and should be designed as *slow flow* (seconds to tens of seconds), not as an action game.
  - **Typing under pressure**: if the NPC speaks while the user is writing, the draft survives (B2) and "the player began to answer" is recorded only if sent.

## 3. Options

### Option A. Minimum: speech-paced stream + interrupt button + reaction taps
- **How:** playback queue (2.1), interruption (decided), plus a small reaction palette that writes typed events. NPC calls receive recent reactions as observed behaviour.
- **Strengths:** small, directly extends the decided design, gives the "listening with a face" feel. No new LLM call types.
- **Weaknesses:** no sense of a living background; quick actions still need typing.
- **Cost:** reactions are 0 extra calls; they enter the next NPC call's context (a few tokens).

### Option B. A + hotspots and quick actions from the panel
- **How:** the client marks entities in text from the PC's perceived-entity list; tapping offers a context menu built from what the PC can do to that entity (a typed list, derived from records, not generated). Panel buttons emit typed actions.
- **Strengths:** removes most typing for common actions; preserves the leak boundary; works well on a phone.
- **Weaknesses:** needs entity linking in narration (the narrator output must carry entity ids or the extractor must resolve mentions, a validation step). Menus built from records can be dull; combine with B2's intent menu for creative options.
- **Cost:** entity linking: 0 extra calls if the narrator/extractor already emit ids; otherwise 1 small call per narration.

### Option C. B + ambient events and an event "director"
- **How:** a world-level step proposes ambient events with a plausibility judgment and a **budget** (max one per N seconds/scene, scaled by scene type); a rule picks quiet or busy. The narrator weaves them in. In flowing mode, the director also decides when silence has gone long enough that the world moves on.
- **Strengths:** the world feels alive; the director controls pacing instead of random noise. [Found] Left 4 Dead's AI Director is the canonical example of pacing by a director that watches the player's state and alternates tension and relief ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_4_Dead), [interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/q-a-valve-s-swift-on-i-left-4-dead-2-i-s-production-ai-boost)).
- **Weaknesses:** needs a notion of "scene tension" that does not exist yet; easy to overdo; events must be recorded and be perceivable by NPCs too (they would also hear the bell), which adds records.
- **Cost:** 1-2 small Jev calls per scene tick (is something plausible now; what kind), plus narrator tokens. In flowing mode, per silence tick.

### Option D. C + beat-based scene structure (Façade-like)
- **How:** scenes are made of small interruptible **beats**; a beat selector picks the next beat from what happened and where the scene should go; beats can be interrupted by, and woven with, others. [Found] Façade does this with a hand-authored beat library and a drama manager, and handles free-text input mid-beat ([ISEA archive entry](https://isea-archives.siggraph.org/?p=42051), [AAAI paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/18733/18733-52-22385-1-10-20210928.pdf)).
- **Strengths:** a principled answer to "what happens when the player interrupts?" (the beat model already treats interruption as normal).
- **Weaknesses:** the engine is world-agnostic and story-open; beats would be generated, not authored. Heavy new design; not needed for the first prototype.
- **Cost:** high design cost; per-beat selection call.

### Reading the options
- If **a quick prototype of the decided interaction** matters most: A.
- If **phone play with little typing** matters most: B.
- If **the world feeling alive during silence** matters most (flowing mode): C.
- If **long-term story structure** matters most: D, later; it is not a first-prototype item.
- B and C are independent of each other; both build on A.

## 4. Prior art
- [Found] **Façade** (Mateas and Stern, 2005): natural-language input, interruptible and interwoven beats, drama manager ([ISEA](https://isea-archives.siggraph.org/?p=42051), [Grand Text Auto episode](https://grandtextauto.soe.ucsc.edu/2008/03/17/ep-85-facade), [AAAI paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/18733/18733-52-22385-1-10-20210928.pdf)).
- [Found] **Versu** (Short and Evans): character actions driven by social practices that make the next sensible move for each character ([IFWiki](https://www.ifwiki.org/Versu), [AAAI paper](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12616/12616-52-16133-1-2-20201228.pdf)). Relevant to NPC-initiated beats.
- [Found] **Left 4 Dead AI Director**: pacing via tension/relief, cues through music and character barks ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_4_Dead)).
- [Found] **Full-duplex spoken dialogue research** (Moshi and successors): barge-in, backchannels and turn-taking as model abilities ([Moshi model card](https://huggingface.co/kyutai/moshika-rl-seamless), [FLEXI benchmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22243), [SALMONN-omni](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17060)). Relevant only if voice conversation becomes a mode; for this engine's pipeline (separate calls per NPC) it is not the architecture, but its evaluation axes (pause handling, turn-taking, backchanneling, interruption) are a checklist for flowing mode.
- [Found] **AI Dungeon**: retry stack and editing ([help](https://help.aidungeon.io/faq/how-to-play)).
- Own study: Concordia's "interrupt" is attention masking, not utterance cutting (`reports/code_study/concordia.md`); nothing studied implements retroactive interruption.

## 5. Interactions
- **Decisions:** interruption, rollback, unsaid remainder, two time modes, "player sees only the story" (see Possible conflicts in the summary: the panel, hotspots and reaction palette are interface beyond text).
- **B1:** posture, hands, quick actions.
- **B2:** drafts survive interruptions; intent menu and quick actions can share a UI.
- **B4:** voice defines the playback clock; music cues reflect ambient/tension state.
- **B5:** pace, skip, silence timer, ambient frequency.
- **Session A:** A1 (ambient events are the visible surface of rumours and world activity), A4 (NPC-initiated beats come from off-screen agents' goals), A6 (time modes, travel, waiting).
