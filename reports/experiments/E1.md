# E1. Knowledge leakage: instructed (A) vs filtered (B)

**Question.** Does a character leak a fact it should not know when the fact sits in its context with a "this character does not know this" instruction (A), compared with the fact being absent (B)?

## Setup (what was actually run)

- **Cases:** 39 in `experiments/e1/cases.jsonl` (generator `make_cases.py`): 15 *secret* (another character's secret), 8 *elsewhere* (event the character was not present for), 6 *future* (later event), and 10 *invented* (campaign-specific facts about Ragnar, Vesemir, Eskel etc. that exist in no book). Scenes are my own short paraphrases, not book excerpts. The *invented* group is a control: its facts cannot be in any model's pretraining data, so any B-condition "leak" there is pure chance or confabulation. For the 29 canon-derived cases, B can also "leak" through pretraining knowledge of the saga.
- **Conditions:** A = system prompt contains the fact plus "{character} does not know this, do not use it". B = same prompt without the fact. The user turn probes for the fact.
- **Probes (two sets, because the first set turned out to be flawed):** *leading* probes (`probe`) mention the topic of the fact in the question ("Is it true that Philippa is playing her own game?"); *neutral* probes (`probe_neutral`) are open questions that do not contain the fact ("What do you know about Philippa?"). I wrote the leading set first. The first run showed that B leaked **more** than A, which I traced to leading probes (see below), so I added the neutral set and ran it as the main measurement.
- **Narrator models (4 tiers):** `qwen/qwen3.5-9b` (small), `openai/gpt-5.4-mini` (mid), `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` (mid2), `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` (strong). Temperature 0.8. Leading set: 2 samples per cell (624 generations). Neutral set: 1 sample per cell (312 generations).
- **Judges:** first pass `openai/gpt-5.4-mini` with verdicts leak / hint / none / meta. All rows it flagged leak or hint in the neutral set were re-judged by `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5` with a stricter instruction (`adjudicate.py`). Headline number = **both judges say "leak"**.
- **Manual check:** I read (a) all 16 rows the first judge marked *leak* in the neutral set and (b) a random 16 (5 flagged-A, 5 flagged-B, 6 none) in the neutral set.

Assumptions I made: "narrator model" = the model that voices the character reply; one reply is 2–5 sentences; the judge sees the fact, a one-line key, the probe, and the reply.

Paired by (case, tier): A-only leak 2, B-only leak 6, both 0, out of 156 pairs. A sign test on 8 discordant pairs gives p ≈ 0.29, so **A and B are not distinguishable at this sample size**; the data are compatible with anything from "A leaks a bit more" to "B leaks several times more".

What the leaks were:

- **A (2 confirmed):** one is `c21` (Philippa: "Radovid hunts sorcerers, that is no secret", plausible from lore alone); one is a real leak by the small model (`c36`, invented fact: it states that Geralt hid the shield fragments in the stable, which only the instruction text said).
- **B (6 confirmed):** all on canon cases (0/40 on invented cases). Examples: Dandelion saying the queen of Cintra perished when the city fell; Cahir saying "they whispered about a marriage, an ancient blood" about the emperor's interest in Ciri; Philippa describing Radovid's plan to cut down the Lodge. These come from pretraining knowledge of the saga or from lore-plausible guesses. That is a **different failure than leaking a context fact**, but for a game built on a known canon it is just as visible in play: a character "knows" things that it had no channel to learn.

Interpretation: when the *question itself* states the fact, B (fact absent) goes along with the premise 12% of the time (strict) and 36% leak-or-hint, and the invented control shows the same (14/80 strict in B vs 1/80 in A). The model is not reading the fact from context; it is accepting and elaborating a premise supplied by the player. Condition A cuts this because the instruction makes the model deny. So **filtering at retrieval protects against the engine supplying a fact; it does not protect against the player supplying it in the question**. That needs a separate mechanism (for example judging, per NPC line, whether a claim in the line had a channel).

## Judge reliability and manual check

- First judge "leak" precision, from reading all 16 neutral rows it labelled leak: 9 clear matches, 3 borderline, 4 false positives. All 4 false positives were condition A replies in which the character *denied* knowledge, so the first judge inflated A. The strong judge confirmed 8 of the 16 as leak, downgraded 6 to hint and 2 to none.
- First judge "hint" is unreliable: of 8 random hint rows I read, 1 was a real hint. Do not use hint counts as a leakage measure; they are shown only as an upper bound.
- 6 of 6 randomly chosen "none" rows were correct. I agree with the first judge on 9 of 16 rows in the random sample (mostly because of the hint over-flagging); on "leak" rows the agreement is about 56–75%.
- Therefore the "both judges" number is the defensible lower-bound estimate. It undercounts true leaks to the extent both judges miss subtle ones; I did not audit "none" rows beyond the 6 sampled.

## Failure examples

- Small model, A, invented control: asked about the shield, it states "Geralt in the stable fixed his wounds and hid the fragments" — copied from the instruction text despite "do not use".
- Judge over-flagging (not a model failure): haiku-5.5, B, invented case `c32` (mirror under the bed) replied "lift that pillow and look"; the first judge marked it *hint*, I read it as *none*.
- Sonnet in B: "Cahir: they whispered about a marriage, an ancient blood" — lore knowledge, not context.

## Limitations

- 39 cases, 156 neutral pairs: wide confidence intervals (shown). No difference between A and B is established.
- Neutral probes were drafted by an LLM and then rewritten by hand; they still range from very open to moderately pointed. A harder adversarial probe set (multi-turn pressure, "I know you know") was not run.
- One sample per cell in the neutral set, so the leak rates carry sampling noise on top of the judge noise.
- Judges are LLMs; hint is unreliable; subtle partial leaks may be missed by both.
- Canon cases mix context leakage with pretraining knowledge; only the 10 invented cases separate them, and with n=40 per condition they cannot detect rates below ~7%.
- Ukrainian text, saga-specific names, and some cases are loosely canon-faithful (invented in a canon style), because the test needs the fact to be unknowable *in the scene*, not canonically exact.
- Spend: about $2.0 including the second-pass judge, which was more than I planned for this experiment (the strong judge runs with mandatory reasoning and is expensive).
