# E3. State extraction from a narrated turn

**Question.** Which models extract state changes (new facts with witnesses/where/notability, inventory, injuries, relationship shifts) from a narrated turn accurately enough?

## Setup

- **Passages:** 20 in `experiments/e3/passages.jsonl`: 13 from the books (Last Wish, Sword of Destiny, Blood of Elves, Baptism of Fire, Lady of the Lake) and 7 from `Ragnar.md`. The file stores only line ranges into `data/` plus my hand-written gold; passage text is read from `data/` at run time and never committed. Passages are 7–25 non-empty lines each. Mixed difficulty on purpose: dialogue-heavy scenes, a fight, an injury scene, a scene where almost nothing happens (`b10`), and solitary scenes where the only witness is the character (`r07`).
- **Gold:** by hand, 70 facts (free text + where + witnesses + notability), 4 inventory changes, 9 injuries, 1 relationship shift. Gold is deliberately *sparse*: only changes I judged worth storing. Each passage comes with a one-line scene header (my words) that resolves aliases ("the stranger" = Geralt).
- **Models (7, 4 to ~100+B):** `google/gemma-3-4b-it` (4B), `mistralai/ministral-8b-2512` (8B), `qwen/qwen3.5-9b` (9B), `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash`, `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5`, `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`. Temperature 0, JSON mode, reasoning off (Sonnet: low, mandatory). One prompt (`SYS` in `run.py`), no few-shot.
- **Scoring:** `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` matches predicted items to gold by meaning (`score()` in `run.py`), marks unmatched predicted facts as *supported by the passage* or not, and grades witness lists (same / superset / subset / partial / different). Metrics: fact recall = gold facts covered / 70; fact precision = matched predicted facts / predicted facts; **hallucination rate = predicted facts the matcher marked unsupported by the passage / predicted facts**; witness exact = share of matched facts with identical witness list, witness graded = same 1, superset/subset/partial 0.5, different 0. Inventory/injury/relationship F1 are micro-averaged over passages.
- **Cost / latency** are for the extraction call only (the matcher cost about $0.06 for all 140 scorings).

How to read the table:

- **Recall vs precision.** Fact precision is dragged down mainly by *true but un-gold* statements, not by lies: gold lists only the story-relevant facts, so a model that also records "the horses shifted their hooves" or "Geralt picks up the reins" loses precision without being wrong. The separate hallucination column is the measure of invention.
- **Hallucinations** are rare everywhere: 4.1–4.3% for the three small/open models (gemma-4B, ministral-8B, qwen-9B), 0.9% for deepseek-v4-flash, 0% for gemini-lite, haiku and sonnet. At n≈70–120 predicted facts per model that is 1–5 invented facts, so the ordering among the larger models is not established, but "small models invent a little, larger ones almost not at all on short passages" is the visible pattern.
- **Granularity differs wildly.** gemma-4B / ministral-8B / deepseek emit 5.7–6.2 facts per passage including scenery (gemma on `r04` recorded the weather, the river and the horses' hooves as facts); gemini-lite emits 2.4 and misses a lot (recall 0.61) but what it emits is almost always right (precision 0.91). Haiku and Sonnet are in between (3.3–3.5). If the store should keep only story-relevant facts, a prompt that sets granularity, plus a dedup/salience step, matters more than model choice here.
- **Witness lists are the weak point for every model.** Exact agreement with my gold is only 0.23–0.55, graded 0.59–0.77. Over all 413 matched-fact gradings: same 157, subset 122, partial 104, superset 17, different 13 (8 not applicable), i.e. models mostly list fewer witnesses than my gold, not more. I did not categorize the causes. Part of this is my gold being one defensible reading (e.g. whether an inn's patrons witnessed a fight); I did not measure inter-annotator agreement.
- **Injuries** are easy (F1 0.78–0.95, Sonnet 0.95). **Inventory and relationships** are not measurable with this set: only 4 gold inventory changes and 1 relationship shift, while models add many plausible-but-unlisted entries (e.g. five of seven models recorded a "Geralt -> Regis: worse" shift in `b07` after the sword-to-throat moment, which I did not put in gold; arguably they are right and my gold is incomplete). Treat those two F1 columns as anecdotes. Sonnet's higher scores there (0.50, 0.33) come from adding far fewer extra entries (2 and 4 FPs vs 30 and 26 for qwen-9B), not from a demonstrated gain in finding true ones.
- **Notability** and **where** were collected but not scored (the matcher was not asked to grade them), so I make no claim about them.
- **Cost:** per 100 turns of this size: gemma-4B $0.005, qwen-9B $0.016, ministral-8B $0.021, deepseek $0.029, haiku $0.033, gemini-lite $0.062, Sonnet $0.633. Latency p50: gemini-lite 1.2 s, gemma 2.1 s, haiku 2.6 s, Sonnet 3.5 s, deepseek 4.3 s, qwen 4.9 s, ministral 5.7 s. Short outputs; longer turns will scale up.
- **JSON validity** was 20/20 for every model in JSON mode.

## Matcher reliability

I read the matcher's output for four passage/model pairs (b09/ministral, r04/gemma, b02/haiku, b06/qwen; 25 predicted facts) and disagreed with 2 fact-level decisions: in b02 it matched a predicted fact to the wrong gold fact (costing that model recall), and in b06 it marked a weakly-supported statement ("Geralt realises the shooter cannot be seen") as unsupported (adding one to that model's hallucination count). The witness grades I checked in those four pairs looked right. This is a small check, not an agreement statistic. The first matcher run also failed to return JSON in 5/40 cases; `score()` now retries with a stricter instruction (0 unscored in the final run). No second matcher model was run.

## Failure examples

- Gemma-4B on `r04` (gate opens) listed 13 "facts", 9 of them scenery or posture, and the 4 gold facts were all found among them (recall full, precision 0.31).
- Qwen-9B on `b06`: recorded the invisible-archer statement as a separate fact ("Geralt discovered the shooter is invisible") that the passage does not state; matcher marked it unsupported.
- Haiku on `b02`: wrote a fact that the mayor acknowledged people vanished near the dam; the matcher mapped it onto the "no reward" gold fact instead of the "killed on the dam" gold fact, so one gold fact was counted as missed.
- Witness lists on `b07` and `b09`: only 1 of the 14 model/passage pairs (qwen-9B on `b07`, one matched fact) had all matched facts graded "same"; most grades were subset or partial, i.e. models listed fewer witnesses than my gold or a different set. I did not categorize causes beyond that.

## Limitations

- 20 passages, 70 gold facts, one annotator (me). Granularity and witness choices are judgement calls; there is no inter-annotator check, so precision/witness numbers carry that bias.
- Gold is sparse by design; precision therefore penalizes legitimate extra facts. Use the hallucination column for invention, and do not compare the precision column to a dense-gold benchmark.
- Inventory/injury/relationship categories have too few gold items (4/9/1) to rank models. Passages with many item changes were rare in the sources I sampled and I did not hunt for them.
- Single prompt, temperature 0, one run per model: no variance estimate and no prompt tuning. A small model with a tighter granularity instruction and few-shot examples would probably look better than here; Sonnet with the same prompt would not improve much on precision.
- The matcher is an LLM from the same vendor as two candidate models; no second matcher or human re-scoring beyond the 29 facts I checked.
- Short passages (≤ 25 lines). Long, multi-event turns and the interaction with the existing campaign store (deduplication against known facts) were not tested.
- Ukrainian text with saga names; results may differ in other languages.
